# Work log

Author: Tarun Verma

Dated record of the work and its findings.

## 2026-10-21

- Seven candidate model pairs were selected and the laptop was checked (RTX 5070 Laptop GPU, 8 GB, torch 2.11 with CUDA 12.8). No CUDA toolkit, C++ build tools, CMake, Ninja, ffmpeg or COLMAP were installed at the start. They were installed with winget and verified by version (CUDA 12.8, MSVC 14.44, CMake 4.4.4, Ninja 1.13.2, ffmpeg 9.0.2, COLMAP 4.2.1). The first installer run failed silently because of a script error and was repeated. Hugging Face licences for the gated models were accepted.
- The repository and the shared evaluation framework (`docs/EVALUATION_FRAMEWORK.md`) were created.
- Pair 2 (3D reconstruction): the build of gsplat on Windows with torch 2.11 failed three times (the Windows `small` macro, a changed private torch API, a GCC-only compiler flag); each cause was found and fixed, and a smoke test passed. The nerfacc CUDA kernels do not compile against torch 2.11 under MSVC (error C2872), so nerfacto runs in pure PyTorch (0.227 s per iteration in the smoke test). The Hugging Face copy of the poster scene holds 100 of the 226 images listed in its transforms file, so a file listing only the existing frames was created.
- Pair 4 (web agents): the MiniWoB++ episode timer runs in real time (10 s), so a correct action after a 12 s delay scored -1.0 and, with the limit raised to 300 s, 0.96. The evaluation therefore uses the extended limit and measures latency separately. A quick test showed the vision agent scoring 0 of 4 because Ollama silently rescales small images and Qwen2.5-VL reports coordinates in that rescaled space; a calibration with a red square at known positions led to screenshot scale 8 (mean click error 3.6 page pixels). The full run (360 episodes) gave a success rate of 23% for the random agent, 80% for the text agent and 73% for the vision agent (paired McNemar p = 0.31), and the injected instruction was followed in 53% (text) and 7% (vision) of 30 episodes each.
- Pair 6 (vision-language models): the full run (700 answers per model) gave POPE adversarial accuracy of 86.0% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and 87.7% (InternVL3-8B in 4-bit), with hallucination rates of 2.7% and 14.0%. InternVL3 first took 36 to 170 s per answer inside the notebook because its image tiling and 7.6 GB footprint spilled into shared GPU memory; usable GPU memory on this laptop is about 7.8 GB. The tiling was capped at 6 tiles and the output layer was quantised (6.7 GB), after which answers took 1.6 s (median).
- The laptop restarted without a clean shutdown (Windows event 41) during a feasibility test, and again on 22 October at 06:26 during a pair 2 test (free RAM near 0.4 GB). Nothing was lost on disk. A telemetry logger, a memory guard script (`docs/diagnostics/guard.ps1`, which stops this project's jobs when commit headroom falls below 1.0 GB) and a fixed 32 GB pagefile (set at 23:45 on 21 October; the memory guard had tripped once at 23:36 with 0.92 GB of headroom) were added; the telemetry logger ran from 21:48 on 21 October to 00:08 on 22 October and was restarted at 08:04 on 22 October, and a per-process GPU memory cap of 92 percent is set in the workers.
- Pairs 1 (image editing), 3 (video generation) and 5 (image generation) were also built, run and repeated. They were archived outside the repository on 27 October when the scope was reduced.

## 2026-10-22

- Pair 7 (safety filters): Llama Guard 3-1B and ShieldGemma-2B were run on ToxicChat, HateCheck and a 60-message retail set, with lower-case and leetspeak evasion tests. A review of the first version of the evasion probe found that its HateCheck prompts came from only four functional tests (the first rows of the file). The probe was replaced by a random sample over all tests (150 unsafe and 150 safe prompts per source) with Wilson intervals and paired tests. Under leetspeak the recall of Llama Guard rises (ToxicChat 55 to 79 percent) but its false blocks on harmless prompts rise from 16 to 66 percent, while the recall of ShieldGemma falls (69 to 54 percent).
- Pair 4 and pair 6 results were recomputed from the raw files. Pair 4: success depends on the task (text agent better on 4 tasks, vision agent on 2, 4 tied; task-level bootstrap interval for the difference -0.23 to +0.37) and the injection was followed in 16 of 30 against 2 of 30 episodes (Fisher p = 0.00014). Pair 6: equal accuracy hides different errors (hallucination 2.7 against 14.0 percent, recall 74.7 against 89.3 percent), and the 300 POPE questions come from 136 images. The extra analysis notebooks of pairs 4 and 6 and the completeness script `tools/check_results.py` were added.
- Pair 2 diagnosis: nerfacto on a larger scene froze after 300 to 400 iterations because the periodic evaluation render (32,768 rays per chunk) needed more GPU memory than the card has, and the GPU memory spilled into shared memory. Further causes were found and fixed: the parallel data manager hangs evaluation on Windows (it pickles the whole script), torch 2.6 and later refuse to load nerfstudio checkpoints without `weights_only=False`, and the in-training evaluation of splatfacto divides by a zero timer reading. The fixes are the plain data manager, 4,096 rays per chunk, no in-training evaluation, trusted checkpoint loading, and a Windows job-object limit of 16 GB of committed memory for every pair 2 job.
- Pair 2 on the poster scene (nerfacto 2,500 iterations, splatfacto 15,000): PSNR 20.25 against 32.93 dB, SSIM 0.802 against 0.957, LPIPS 0.224 against 0.048, render speed 0.36 against 270 frames per second, training energy 14.1 against 3.8 Wh (10 held-out views). The quality-against-effort curve was added: nerfacto 2,500, 5,000 and 10,000 iterations (PSNR 20.2, 20.3, 20.1 dB) and splatfacto 1,000, 3,000, 7,000 and 15,000 iterations (21.8, 19.5, 31.0, 32.9 dB). The larger scene was dropped because nerfacto did not fit in memory.
- My own chair video (two phone videos of 42 s and 34 s) was processed: 188 frames, 6 blurry frames removed, COLMAP on the CPU registered 182 of 182 frames (16,914 points, mean reprojection error 0.58 px); the low circle (101 frames) was used for training and the high circle (81 frames) for testing (`pairs/p2_3d_reconstruction/src/prepare_chair_video.py`). Result on 20 test views: nerfacto 19.98 dB (SSIM 0.668, LPIPS 0.242) against splatfacto 25.01 dB (0.844, 0.144).
- Pair 6 incident: the default Qwen3-VL-8B tag is a reasoning variant. It first gave empty answers, and with an empty reasoning block the open answers were reasoning text cut off by the token limit (strict VQA 0.20). Those rows were moved to `results/feasibility/qwen3_reasoning_variant/` and the instruct variant (`qwen3-vl:8b-instruct`) was used instead (700 answers, none empty, POPE accuracy 86.7 percent). Quick mode also overwrote the shared evaluation manifest and broke the full run; the manifest was rebuilt and checked, and quick mode now writes to its own folder.
- The report and presentation generators were written, so that every number is read from the result files; the slides were exported to images and checked one by one.

- Viewer check of pair 2: both chair-video models were opened in the nerfstudio viewer and rotated, zoomed and tilted (`results/feasibility/viewer_check/`). No frame rate was read from the viewer.
- Correction: the dip of splatfacto at 3,000 iterations had been attributed to nerfstudio's opacity reset. The saved checkpoints do not support this: 93% of the Gaussians of the 3,000-iteration models have an opacity above 0.2 (a reset caps every opacity at 0.2), their mean opacity (0.85 and 0.87) equals that of the 7,000-iteration models (0.87), and the installed gsplat reset condition (`step % reset_every == 0 & step > 0`) is never true. The cause of the dip was not isolated (`results/feasibility/opacity_check/`). A nerfacto run of 30,000 iterations would take about 1.7 to 1.9 hours and about 150 to 170 Wh per scene and seed (from the speed of the 10,000 and the 2,500 iteration runs), which did not fit the time budget, so the curve stops at 10,000.
